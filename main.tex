\begin{abstract}
Drift alarms rarely name the type of drift, although the right response depends on it. In our pipeline every decision is a hypothesis test with family wise error control. Label free tests and black box shift estimation separate covariate from prior shift. A small audit of late labels separates concept drift from label noise. On injected drift in two census tasks and two model families it names the type in \res{bench-a-ours-min} to \res{bench-a-ours-max}\% of windows and beats rules from monitoring practice. A learned meta classifier wins on the mechanisms it was trained on but reaches only \res{bench-b-meta-min} to \res{bench-b-meta-max}\% on held out mechanisms, where the tests keep \res{bench-b-ours-min} to \res{bench-b-ours-max}\%. On census data from 2014 to 2023, an aging gas sensor array and an image stream, it reported no drift on any control window and traced documented events. Accuracy estimates, per prediction reliability and triage without labelled history remain weak.
\end{abstract}

\keywords{data drift, concept drift, label shift, model monitoring, hypothesis testing, drift diagnosis, performance estimation}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A deployed model can lose accuracy as production data move away from the training data. The loss usually starts long before reliable labels arrive \cite{sculley2015hidden,paleyes2022challenges}. Monitoring must therefore detect the change and judge its effect while labels are missing.

Monitoring tools report that a distribution changed. They say little about how much accuracy was lost, why it was lost or what to do next \cite{rabanser2019failing}. The answer to the last question depends on the type of drift. A covariate shift may be corrected by reweighting. A prior shift calls for recalibration to the new class balance. Concept drift needs fresh labels. Label noise points to the labelling process rather than the model. Naming the type is thus the step that turns an alarm into an action.

This paper studies how well the drift type can be named from label free tests and a small audit of late labels. Every decision in the pipeline is a hypothesis test at one significance level with family wise error control, so the false alarm rate is fixed in advance and no threshold is tuned on evaluation data. The pipeline also estimates accuracy, localizes and explains the shift, scores each prediction and advises on remediation.

The contributions are as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item A hierarchical test procedure that names the drift type. Without labels it separates covariate shift from prior shift by black box shift estimation and a label shift consistency test. With a few hundred late labels it separates concept drift from label noise by a test of how the pattern of errors changed.
\item A benchmark with a known ground truth on two tabular tasks and two model families. It has two designs. Design A injects drift in ways that partly match the tests. Design B uses held out mechanisms that the tests were not built around, fixed before it was first run.
\item A quantitative comparison with three rule based baselines from monitoring practice and the label shift literature, a learned meta classifier and ablations, with paired tests over replicates.
\item An open end to end pipeline evaluated on three real drifts with known causes: nine years of census data, a gas sensor array that ages over three years and a temporal image stream.
\end{enumerate}
Section~\ref{sec:negative} reports the parts that did not work: accuracy estimation, per prediction reliability, triage without labelled history and a concept probe for images.

Sections~\ref{sec:sota} to \ref{sec:setup} review related work, describe the method and give the setup. Section~\ref{sec:results} reports the results and Sections~\ref{sec:negative} to \ref{sec:conclusion} discuss limits and conclude.

\section{State of the art}
\label{sec:sota}

\paragraph{Drift detection.} Surveys of concept drift cover detection and adaptation in data streams \cite{gama2014survey}. Shift is usually split into covariate shift, prior shift and concept shift \cite{moreno2012unifying}. Detectors that track the error rate, such as DDM \cite{gama2004ddm} and adaptive windowing \cite{bifet2007learning}, need labels. Label free detectors compare inputs or model outputs. Rabanser et al. compare univariate tests, kernel tests, classifier two sample tests and black box shift detection on model outputs (BBSD) and find that BBSD works best overall \cite{rabanser2019failing,lopezpaz2017c2st,gretton2012kernel}. These methods say that a shift happened but not of which type.

\paragraph{Label shift and drift type.} Black box shift estimation (BBSE) recovers a new class prior from the confusion matrix of any classifier, assuming the inputs within each class do not change \cite{lipton2018bbse}. Liu et al. show that changes in $P(Y\mid X)$ dominate many real tabular shifts \cite{liu2023whyshift}. Kulinski and Inouye explain a shift by a transport map between distributions \cite{kulinski2023explaining}. Both analyses need labels or full access to both samples. To our knowledge no published method names all four types, covariate, prior, concept and label noise, from label free tests plus a small label audit, with a stated error rate.

\paragraph{Performance estimation without labels.} Average confidence \cite{hendrycks2017baseline}, difference of confidences (DoC) \cite{guillory2021predicting}, average thresholded confidence (ATC) \cite{garg2022atc} and confidence based performance estimation (CBPE) \cite{kivimaki2025performance} estimate accuracy from model outputs. All assume that calibration carries over to production, which fails under shift \cite{ovadia2019trust}.

\paragraph{Reliability, explanation and remediation.} Models stay confident as accuracy falls under shift \cite{ovadia2019trust}. Out of distribution (OOD) scores such as the Isolation Forest \cite{liu2008isolation} flag unusual inputs. Shapley additive explanations (SHAP) \cite{lundberg2017unified} and class activation maps (CAM) \cite{zhou2016cam} attribute predictions to features or image regions. Importance weighting corrects covariate shift \cite{shimodaira2000,sugiyama2007,cortes2010weights}.

\paragraph{Benchmarks.} Folktables \cite{ding2021retiring}, TableShift \cite{gardner2023tableshift} and CLEAR \cite{lin2021clear} provide real shifts without a ground truth for their type. Injected shifts give that ground truth \cite{rabanser2019failing} but risk matching the method under test, which our design B addresses.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} shows the pipeline. One principle runs through every stage. Each decision is a hypothesis test at a significance level $\alpha=\res{acs-alpha}$, with the family wise error rate controlled by the Holm procedure \cite{holm1979} or its Bonferroni bound.

\subsection{Data, representation and reference split}
Nominal features, such as an occupation code, are declared categorical because their numeric order has no meaning. Images are mapped to the \res{clear-dim} dimensional global average pool output of a frozen ImageNet residual network with 18 layers (ResNet-18) \cite{he2016deep}, so image drift can reuse the tabular tests \cite{rabanser2019failing}.

The labelled reference data are split into three disjoint parts, stratified by class: \res{acs-split-pct-train}\% for training, \res{acs-split-pct-calibration}\% for calibration and \res{acs-split-pct-test}\% for testing. The training part fits the model. The calibration part never enters training. It is the reference window for every detector and fits the accuracy estimator and the reliability fusion. The test part gives the reference accuracy and serves as a control window that should raise no alarm.

\subsection{Detection, estimation and localization}
Six detectors decide whether the inputs or the model outputs moved. The Kolmogorov--Smirnov (KS) test runs on each numeric feature, with a Bonferroni correction. The population stability index (PSI) alarms at the usual convention of \res{acs-psi-threshold} \cite{siddiqi2006}. Maximum mean discrepancy (MMD) \cite{gretton2012kernel} is a permutation test on standardized inputs with a Gaussian kernel. The confidence test compares histograms of the top class confidence with a $\chi^2$ test of homogeneity, pooling sparse cells until every expected count reaches \res{const-min-expected} \cite{cochran1954}. Two detectors from Rabanser et al. serve as baselines \cite{rabanser2019failing}. BBSD runs a KS test on each class probability of the model. The domain classifier is a classifier two sample test \cite{lopezpaz2017c2st}: gradient boosted trees learn to tell \res{acs-dc-n} reference rows from as many production rows. The held out accuracy is compared with $1/2$ by an exact binomial test. Every permutation p value is $(1+b)/(1+B)$, where $b$ of $B$ permutations reach the observed statistic \cite{phipson2010}.

Accuracy is estimated without labels by CBPE \cite{kivimaki2025performance}. A map $a(\cdot)$ from confidence to the probability of a correct prediction is fitted by histogram binning \cite{zadrozny2001} on the calibration part. For a window with confidences $c_1,\dots,c_n$,
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\mathrm{acc}} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} a(c_i).
\label{eq:cbpe}
\end{equation}
The estimate is unbiased only while the probability of a correct prediction at a given confidence stays as in the calibration data. Average confidence, DoC and ATC with the negative entropy score are fitted on the same calibration part as baselines \cite{hendrycks2017baseline,guillory2021predicting,garg2022atc}.

Localization tests every feature with KS (numeric) or $\chi^2$ homogeneity (nominal) under Holm correction. It reports an effect size in $[0,1]$, the KS statistic or Cram\'er's $V$. For nominal features it also gives the share of production rows whose category never occurs in the reference.

\subsection{Drift type}
\label{sec:diagnosis}
The type is named in two stages. Figure~\ref{fig:decision} gives the decision path.

\paragraph{Label free stage.} Three block tests share one Holm procedure at level $\alpha$: all feature tests pooled by their Bonferroni p value, a $\chi^2$ test on the predicted labels and the MMD test. Individual features are tested only if the pooled block rejects, within the block and at the level at which the block was rejected. This gatekeeping keeps the family wise error rate at $\alpha$ \cite{holm1979}. It lets a permutation test, whose smallest p value is $1/(B+1)$, compete with many feature tests. If no block rejects, the label free stage reports no shift.

If a block rejects, BBSE \cite{lipton2018bbse} estimates the new class prior. With $C_{jk}=P(\hat y=j,y=k)$ on the calibration part and $\mu_j$ the share of production predictions in class $j$, the class weights solve
\begin{equation}
C\,w = \mu, \qquad w_k = q(y=k)/p(y=k).
\label{eq:bbse}
\end{equation}
Under a pure prior shift the distribution of inputs within each class is unchanged. Reweighting each reference row by $w_{y}$ must then reproduce the production inputs. The feature tests are repeated against the reweighted reference, using Kish's effective sample size \cite{kish1965}, with Holm correction. A feature that still differs carries a covariate shift. If none differs and the predicted labels moved, the shift is a prior shift. A prior shift always moves the predicted labels of an informative classifier, so a shift without that move is called covariate.

\paragraph{Audit stage.} Concept drift and label noise leave the inputs and model outputs unchanged, so no label free test can see them. The audit uses $m$ late labels. A one sided two proportion test asks whether accuracy fell. If it did, a likelihood ratio test checks each feature $f$. A logistic model of whether a prediction is correct is fitted on the calibration rows and the audit rows together. The null model holds \res{const-quantile-bins} confidence bins at reference quantiles, bins of $f$, an audit indicator and its interaction with the confidence bins. The alternative adds the interaction of the audit indicator with the bins of $f$. Uniform label noise at rate $r$ turns $P(\mathrm{correct}\mid x)$ into $r+(1-2r)P(\mathrm{correct}\mid x)$, a function of confidence alone, which the null absorbs. A feature significant after Holm correction means the target rule moved in a region of that feature: concept drift. No significant feature means label noise.

\subsection{Root cause analysis}
For tabular models the pipeline computes SHAP values \cite{lundberg2017unified} of the deployed model with its preprocessing. It compares the mean absolute attribution of each feature between reference and production rows. Each change carries a percentile bootstrap interval \cite{efron1993} whose level is Bonferroni adjusted over the features. For images, the monitored head is linear in the pooled feature maps $A_j(u,v)$ of the last convolutional block, so for class $k$ with coefficients $w_{kj}$ and feature scales $s_j$ the class activation map
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{CAM}_k(u,v) = \sum_j \frac{w_{kj}}{s_j}\, A_j(u,v)
\label{eq:cam}
\end{equation}
averages to the class logit up to a constant \cite{zhou2016cam}. Both explanations need the logistic model. SHAP for the boosted model would take hours per window, since the exact explainer over ten inputs evaluates 1{,}024 coalitions per explained row. CAM needs a linear head. Both are therefore reported for the logistic model only.

\subsection{Reliability and remediation triage}
Each prediction receives three signals in $[0,1]$: confidence risk $1-c$, an OOD score from an Isolation Forest \cite{liu2008isolation} with a standardized distance and instability under small input noise. A logistic regression fitted on the calibration part, with an indicator of a wrong prediction as the target, combines them into a probability of error.

The triage predicts, without production labels, whether a cheap retrain will help. A classifier that tells reference rows from production rows gives cross fitted density ratio weights, truncated at \res{const-weight-clip} times their mean \cite{cortes2010weights}. A model trained with these weights is compared with an unweighted one on a held out \res{const-triage-valfrac}\% of the reference, weighted by the density ratio \cite{shimodaira2000,sugiyama2007}. When late labels from an earlier window exist, a second probe retrains on them and is validated on a held out part of that window. A retrain is recommended when a probe's predicted gain is significant after Holm correction across probes.

\textbf{ACS Income and ACS Employment.} The two Folktables tasks \cite{ding2021retiring} predict whether a person earns more than \$50{,}000 (\res{inc-nfeatures} features) and whether a person aged 16 to 90 is employed (\res{emp-nfeatures} features). California 2014 is the reference. The temporal windows are California 2015 to 2019 and 2021 to 2023. There is no standard 2020 release \cite{census2021experimental}. The geographic windows are Texas, New York, Florida and Washington in 2014. From 2019 the relationship variable RELP was replaced by RELSHIPP \cite{census2019pums}, which we map back to RELP by the code labels of the 2018 and 2019 data dictionaries. The nine years hold five documented changes: new occupation codes in 2018 \cite{census2020occupation}, a redesigned relationship question in 2019, new coding of race answers from 2020 \cite{census2021race}, the pandemic and wage growth against the fixed income threshold.

\textbf{Gas sensor array drift.} The UCI dataset holds 13{,}910 measurements of six gases by 16 metal oxide sensors over 36 months, in ten batches \cite{vergara2012gas,rodriguezlujan2014gas}. Each measurement has eight features per sensor. The sensors age, so their response to the same gas drifts while the labels stay exact: a documented change in $P(X\mid Y)$. Batches 1 and 2 form the reference and batches 3 to 10 the windows. Some batches are small, so the audit takes $\min(600,\lfloor n/2\rfloor)$ labels.

\textbf{CLEAR-10.} Ten object classes and a background class in ten time ordered buckets of about 3{,}300 images each \cite{lin2021clear}. Bucket 1 is the reference.

In every dataset the held out test part of the reference is a control window.

\subsection{Models}
Two untuned model families are monitored by the same pipeline. The first is logistic regression (LR) with $L_2$ regularization ($C=1$) on standardized numeric and one hot encoded nominal features. The second is histogram gradient boosting (GB) \cite{friedman2001greedy,ke2017lightgbm} from scikit-learn \cite{pedregosa2011scikit}, with default settings, no early stopping and native splits on nominal codes. Codes beyond the 254 most frequent are pooled, which is the limit of the implementation. For CLEAR-10 both act on the frozen embeddings.

\subsection{Injection benchmark}
\label{sec:benchmark-setup}
Real drift has no ground truth for its type, so the type is graded on injected drift. The benchmark uses the 2014 data of \res{bench-nstates} states (\res{bench-states}) for both ACS tasks. Each state is a reference distribution with its own model, split as above. For each of \res{bench-nseeds} seeds the split, the window and the injection are redrawn, which gives \res{bench-nreps} replicates per configuration. Each replicate draws a production window of \res{bench-prodsize} rows and a grading holdout of \res{bench-holdsize} rows from the test part. Each mechanism is injected at three intensities into both.

\textbf{Design A} injects the four types in ways that partly match the tests. Covariate shift rescales two numeric features (ACS Income: \res{bench-cov-features-inc}) for a random subset of rows. Prior shift drops rows of one class until the positive rate rises by \res{bench-prior-steps}. Concept drift inverts the labels of the \res{bench-concept-steps}\% of rows with the highest value of one feature (ACS Income: \res{bench-concept-feature-inc}). Label noise flips a random \res{bench-noise-steps}\% of labels. A control window without drift completes each replicate.

\textbf{Design B} uses held out mechanisms that the tests were not built around. Covariate shift is selection bias: each row is kept with probability $\sigma(\beta s(x))$, where $s$ is a seeded random score over all inputs and $\beta\in\{\res{bench-b-betas}\}$. The rows stay real and $P(Y\mid X)$ is exactly unchanged. Prior shift lowers the positive rate by \res{bench-b-prior-steps}. Concept drift inverts the labels in the top \res{bench-b-concept-steps}\% of a second random score, an oblique region that no single feature defines. For ACS Income a second concept drift raises the income threshold to \res{bench-b-thresholds}. Label noise is class conditional: positive labels are flipped to negative at rates of \res{bench-b-noise-steps}\%.

Each window is graded against its recipe. The audit uses \res{bench-audit-n} labels and is repeated with \res{bench-audit-sizes} labels. Proportions carry Wilson intervals \cite{wilson1927} and means bootstrap intervals over replicates.

\subsection{Baselines and ablations}
\label{sec:baselines}
Every method names one of the five types from the same test results and the same audit labels. \emph{Input monitoring} reads any input alarm as covariate shift, which is how drift alarms are commonly read. \emph{BBSE first} reads a shift of the predicted labels as prior shift \cite{lipton2018bbse,rabanser2019failing} and any other alarm as covariate shift. It is our label free stage without the consistency test. \emph{Error rate monitoring} adds a significant audit accuracy drop read as concept drift, as an error rate detector would \cite{gama2004ddm}. The \emph{learned meta classifier} is GB on nine statistics of each window: the evidence $-\log_{10}p$ of each block test, the share of features with residual shift, the BBSE prior change, the CBPE change, the evidence and size of the audit accuracy drop and the evidence of the error structure test. In design A it is validated leaving one state out. In design B it is trained on design A, again leaving out the predicted state, so it never sees a held out mechanism. Three ablations remove the MMD block, remove the confidence term of the audit null, or replace the confidence bins by cells of predicted class and confidence.

Ours is compared with each other method by the per replicate difference in the share of correctly typed windows, with a sign flip permutation test (\res{bench-nflips} flips) and Holm correction. Replicates are the unit, since the windows of one replicate share a model and a reference.

\subsection{Configuration}
The significance level is $\alpha=\res{acs-alpha}$ throughout, PSI alarms at \res{acs-psi-threshold} and permutation tests use \res{acs-mmd-perm} permutations. SHAP uses \res{acs-shap-bg} background rows and explains \res{acs-shap-n} rows per side. The real audits use \res{acs-audit-n} labels, drawn from a random stream of their own for each window. All randomness is seeded and results repeat exactly. Everything ran on one workstation with a \res{const-cpu-cores} core processor and one graphics processor. The graphics processor extracted the image embeddings and ran the permutation tests in double precision with the same seeded permutations as the processor code. Both paths gave identical p values in our checks. On the CA 2023 ACS Income window of \res{inc-ca2023-n} rows the KS pipeline with SHAP takes \res{inc-ca2023-time-ks} seconds and the diagnosis \res{inc-ca2023-time-diagnosis} seconds. The \res{bench-total-windows} benchmark windows took \res{bench-total-hours} hours. The software was scikit-learn \cite{pedregosa2011scikit}, SciPy, NumPy, SHAP, OpenCLIP and PyTorch.

Each of them can name only two to four of the five types. Over all eight configurations ours is significantly better in \res{bench-nbetter} of the \res{bench-ntests} comparisons with a baseline or the learned rule and significantly worse in \res{bench-nworse}, \res{bench-worse-cells}.

The learned meta classifier is the strongest competitor on design A. Validated on states it has not seen, it reaches \res{b-inc-a-lr-metaclassifier-acc}\% on ACS Income, above our \res{b-inc-a-lr-ours-acc}\%. It learns the recipes of design A well. On the held out mechanisms of design B the picture reverses. Trained on design A, the meta classifier falls to \res{b-inc-b-lr-metaclassifier-acc}\% on ACS Income and \res{b-emp-b-lr-metaclassifier-acc}\% on ACS Employment, while ours keeps \res{b-inc-b-lr-ours-acc}\% and \res{b-emp-b-lr-ours-acc}\%. The learned rule had tied the type to the size and shape of the injected shift, for example covariate shift to a large shift of two features, so it misses selection bias that moves many features a little. The tests do not depend on such recipes.

The ablations show which parts matter. Removing the confidence term from the audit null costs \res{b-inc-a-lr-noconfidencenull-diffabs} points on ACS Income design A, because uniform label noise then looks like concept drift. Removing the MMD block changes little on the benchmark (\res{b-inc-a-lr-nommd-diff} points), whose shifts are visible feature by feature. Its value shows on the image embeddings of Section~\ref{sec:res-real}. Replacing the confidence bins by cells of predicted class and confidence helps on ACS Employment design B (\res{b-emp-b-lr-classconfidencenull-acc}\% against \res{b-emp-b-lr-ours-acc}\%) but costs on ACS Income design A (\res{b-inc-a-lr-classconfidencenull-acc}\%). Adopting it now would mean choosing the method after seeing design B, so it remains an ablation and a direction for future work.

\section{Analysis and negative findings}
\label{sec:negative}

Four parts of the pipeline did not work as hoped. We report them because they mark the limits of label free monitoring.

\paragraph{The accuracy estimate is optimistic.} CBPE, like its baselines, assumes that the accuracy at a given confidence stays as in the calibration data. It cannot move under concept drift and label noise. It also misses losses far from the reference. On gas batch 8 the LR model's mean confidence falls from \res{gas-ref-conf} to \res{gas-b8-conf} and its accuracy to \res{gas-b8-acc}, yet CBPE estimates \res{gas-b8-est}: \res{gas-b8-sparse-share}\% of the batch falls in confidence bins that hold only \res{gas-b8-sparse-cal-n} calibration rows, all of them correct. The estimate should be read as an upper bound on accuracy, not as a measurement.

\paragraph{Per prediction reliability reduces to confidence.} On ACS and CLEAR-10 the learned fusion of confidence, OOD score and instability never improves meaningfully on confidence alone. Its AUROC differs from that of confidence by \res{rel-other-diff-min} to \res{rel-other-diff-max} over \res{rel-other-n} windows and by \res{rel-bench-gain-min} to \res{rel-bench-gain-max} on the benchmark. On the gas sensor array the fusion is fitted on only \res{rel-gas-lr-cal-errors} wrong predictions with LR and \res{rel-gas-hgb-cal-errors} with GB among \res{rel-gas-cal-n} calibration rows. It is then unstable and differs from confidence by \res{rel-gas-diff-min} to \res{rel-gas-diff-max}. A learned fusion needs enough errors to learn from.

\paragraph{The remediation triage needs labelled history.} Table~\ref{tab:remediation} grades the triage on every real window, \res{rem-n} settings over both model families. A retrain counts as truly helping when importance weighting (IW) or retraining on the latest labelled window (Recent) raises accuracy significantly on a labelled holdout. Where an earlier window with labels exists, the triage is right in \res{rem-recent-correct} of \res{rem-recent-n} settings, because its recent window probe is validated on real production labels. Where none exists, it relies on importance weighted validation alone. It is then right in \res{rem-norecent-correct} of \res{rem-norecent-n} real settings, mostly by holding, since a retrain truly helped in only \res{rem-norecent-helps} of them. On the benchmark its recall is only \res{b-inc-a-lr-triage-recall}\%. Importance weighted validation cannot see gains that a reweighted model makes by extrapolating outside the reference support, so without labelled history the triage is conservative exactly where drift is strongest.

\section{Threats to validity}
\label{sec:threats}

\paragraph{Construct validity.} Injected drift is cleaner than real drift. Each benchmark window holds one type, while real windows mix several. ACS Income 2023 combines covariate shift with a change in the label rule. The four types are also an idealization. Sensor aging changes $P(X\mid Y)$ and through it $P(Y\mid X)$. Our procedure names it covariate shift because the inputs move in a way a prior change does not explain. Design B reduces, but cannot remove, the risk that the benchmark favours the tests. Its mechanisms were fixed before it was run and the diagnosis was not changed afterwards.

\paragraph{Internal validity.} The weighted residual tests treat the BBSE weights as fixed and ignore their sampling error. With hundreds of thousands of rows small shifts are significant, so effect sizes are reported next to every decision. Comparisons use replicates as the unit.

\paragraph{External validity.} The study covers two tabular tasks from one survey, one sensor dataset, one image stream and two model families. It does not cover deep models trained end to end, text or streaming data.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.} All code and outputs are available\ifanonymous{} (link withheld for review)\else{} in the project repository\fi.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A drift alarm is useful only if it leads to the right action. That action depends on the type of drift. We showed that the type can be named by hypothesis tests with a stated error rate. On injected drift our procedure clearly beats rules from monitoring practice and the label shift literature. A learned meta classifier beats it on the mechanisms it was trained on but fails on held out mechanisms, where the tests keep most of their accuracy. The procedure works unchanged for logistic regression and gradient boosting. On real drift its diagnosis reported no drift on every control window and traced documented events: new occupation codes, a redesigned survey question, new coding of race answers and aging sensors.

The evaluation also marks the limits. Drift that does not reduce accuracy passes the audit unnoticed. Class conditional label noise is mistaken for concept drift. Future work will condition the audit on the full model output, account for the sampling error of the label shift weights, test mixed drift and extend the pipeline to streaming data and deep models.
