Background: Deployed machine learning models lose accuracy when production data drift away from the training data. Monitoring tools raise an alarm when this happens but seldom say what kind of drift occurred, although the right response depends on it: covariate shift calls for reweighting, prior shift for recalibration, concept drift for fresh labels and label noise for a check of the labeling process. This paper presents Data Drift Autopsy, an end-to-end monitoring pipeline that detects drift, estimates the lost accuracy without labels, locates the shifted features, names the drift type, scores the trust due to each prediction and advises whether a cheap retrain will help. At its heart sits a diagnosis made only of hypothesis tests, all held to one family-wise error rate. Its first stage uses no labels and tells covariate shift from prior shift. The second stage is new: given a few hundred late labels, it asks whether the errors have moved in a way the model's own confidence cannot account for, the mark that sets concept drift apart from label noise.

Results: With drift injected into two census tasks and two model families, the diagnosis named the type in 85.7 to 94.3% of windows, ahead of common monitoring rules. A learned meta-classifier did better on drift it had seen, yet fell to 31.1 to 51.2% on held-out drift mechanisms, where our tests still reached 62.4 to 81.5%. On four real datasets with documented changes, the pipeline flagged no control window and pointed to the known events.

Conclusions: Hypothesis tests with a stated error rate can name the drift type, which decides the right response, from label-free tests and a few hundred late labels. Unlike a learned meta-classifier, the procedure carries over to held-out drift, because the tests encode the definitions of the types.
