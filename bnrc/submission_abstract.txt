Background: Deployed machine learning models lose accuracy when production data drift from the training data. Monitoring tools raise an alarm when this happens but seldom say what kind of drift occurred. The right response depends on it, from reweighting for covariate shift to fresh labels for concept drift. This paper presents Data Drift Autopsy, an end-to-end monitoring pipeline that detects drift, estimates the lost accuracy without labels, locates the shifted features and names the drift type.

Methods: The diagnosis consists only of hypothesis tests that share one family-wise error rate. Its first stage uses no labels and tells covariate shift from prior shift. The second stage is new: given 600 late labels, it asks whether the errors have moved in a way the model's own confidence cannot account for, the mark that sets concept drift apart from label noise. We injected drift of known type into two census tasks for two model families, 6500 windows in all, some with mechanisms held out from the design. The pipeline also ran on 41 windows of four real datasets with documented changes.

Results: On injected drift the diagnosis named the type in 85.7 to 94.3% of windows, ahead of common monitoring rules and three published methods. A learned meta-classifier did better on drift it had seen, yet fell to 31.7 to 50.7% on held-out mechanisms, where our tests reached 62.4 to 81.5%. They recognized selection bias, a lower positive rate and oblique concept drift. A raised income threshold, which cost almost no accuracy, was called no drift. Class-conditional label noise was often taken for concept drift. The pipeline's false alarm rate on 160 real control windows was 9.4%, within the stated bound. On real drift it traced the documented causes. Its risk score and retraining advice did no better than model confidence and a simple rule.

Conclusions: Hypothesis tests with a stated error rate can name the drift type from label-free tests and a few hundred late labels and, unlike a learned rule, carry over to held-out drift that costs accuracy. Class-conditional label noise remains hard to tell from concept drift.
